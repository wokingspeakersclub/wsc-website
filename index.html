About us

Everyone starts nervous.

Our members came looking for confidence and left with something much more useful: the ability to speak clearly, think on their feet, and hold a room.

Woking Speakers Club has been running since 2006, part of Toastmasters International — the world's leading organisation for public speaking, with over 300,000 members across 122 countries. We meet at Woking United Reformed Church, two minutes from the town centre.

Our members are teachers, engineers, managers, students and retirees. Some come because they've got a wedding speech looming. Some because a job promotion depends on it. Some just because they're tired of sitting quietly in meetings when they have something to say. All of them find more than they expected.

Visiting is free — come as many times as you like before you decide to join. You won't be asked to speak until you're ready, and you'll leave knowing exactly what the club can do for you.

Meetings

Two hours that go faster than you expect.

Every meeting follows the same shape, but the content — and the laughs — are different every time.

1
Warm up · 15 min
The warm-up
Everyone gets 15 seconds on a shared topic. Guests can simply say "pass" — no one will mind, and no one will notice.
2
Prepared speeches · 30–45 min
Prepared speeches
Three or four members deliver talks they've been working on — anything from a first nervous two-minute speech to a polished, funny, moving ten-minute performance. The range is part of what makes it so compelling to watch.
3
Break · 10 min
The break
Ten minutes to grab a drink, meet other members, and decompress. For first-time visitors, this is the best time to ask questions — everyone is approachable and happy to chat.
4
Evaluations · 20 min
Evaluations
Each speaker receives a structured evaluation from a more experienced member — two minutes of specific, kind, genuinely useful feedback. It's one of the things members say they value most.
5
Table topics · 20 min
Table Topics — speaking off the cuff
A topic is announced and you've got thirty seconds to think before you speak for up to two minutes. Guests are never called upon — but most find themselves quietly desperate to have a go within a visit or two.
🍺
Drinks afterwards
After the meeting most people head to a nearby bar — soft drinks and coffee very much included. It's optional, but this is consistently where first-time visitors decide they're coming back.

Your first visit

Here's exactly what happens.

We know the walk to the door is the hardest part. So here's what to expect, minute by minute.

7:15 pm

Arrive at 7:15 pm

The doors open at 7:15 pm. A member will meet you at the entrance — you won't be standing around wondering where to go or what to do. Someone will introduce themselves and sit with you.

👋

A low-key introduction

The person running the evening (the Toastmaster of the Evening) will briefly mention you're there. That's it. No speech, no spotlight. Just a welcome.

🎤

The warm-up round

The meeting opens with everyone speaking for 15 seconds — including guests, if they want to. "Pass" is a perfectly fine answer. Most first-timers pass and nobody bats an eyelid.

👀

Sit back and watch

You'll see three or four prepared speeches from members at different stages. Some will be rough around the edges. Some will be genuinely remarkable. All of them will show you what's possible.

☕

The interval

Halfway through, there's a ten-minute break. Members will come and find you. Ask anything. Nobody will pressure you to join — people here remember their own first visit too well for that.

🍺

The social afterwards

After the meeting, the group heads to a nearby venue to carry on the conversation over drinks — alcoholic or not, no one's counting. It's optional, but an hour with the members will tell you more about the club than anything on this website.

FAQs

The questions everyone Googles first.

Honest answers to the things people want to know before they feel brave enough to ask.

Do I have to speak on my first visit?

Absolutely not. The most you'll be asked for is your name at the start, and even that's optional. The 15-second warm-up round is open to guests, but "pass" is a perfectly good answer. Nothing else is expected of you until you decide you're ready.

How many times can I come as a guest?

As many times as you need. There's no awkward moment where someone corners you about joining. We'd genuinely rather you come back five times and be certain than rush into something that doesn't fit.

What does membership cost?

Guest visits are always free. Membership is a £20 one-off joining fee, then £13 a month (billed in six-month blocks of £78). For what you get — structured development, mentoring, and a community — most members consider it exceptional value. Full breakdown in the Join section.

How do I join?

Come to a meeting, tell the Vice President Membership you'd like to join, and fill in a membership form. That's genuinely all there is to it. No interview, no audition.

When do I give my first speech?

Usually within your first few meetings as a member — you'll typically be scheduled about four meetings ahead. Need more time? Just say so. Your VP Education will work with you, not against you.

What if I'm terrified of my first speech?

You will be. So was everyone else. Your mentor will help you prepare it, your club will root for you while you're up there, and your evaluator will tell you what was great about it before anything else. First speeches at Woking Speakers are remembered fondly, not cringed at.

Membership

When you're ready, joining takes ten minutes.

No forms to download, no committee to impress. Just turn up, say yes, and you're in.

2

Say you'd like to join

When you're ready, tell the VP Membership or Treasurer. They'll give you a membership form — one page, takes two minutes to complete.
