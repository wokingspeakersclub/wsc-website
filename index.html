About us

Everyone starts nervous.

Our members come looking for confidence and leave with something much more useful: the ability to speak clearly, think on their feet, and hold a room.

Woking Speakers Club has been running since 2006, part of Toastmasters International — the world's leading organisation for public speaking, with over 300,000 members across 122 countries. We meet at Woking United Reformed Church, two minutes from the town centre.

Our members are teachers, engineers, managers, students and retirees. Some come because they've got a wedding speech looming. Some because a job promotion depends on it. Some just because they're tired of sitting quietly in meetings when they have something to say. All of them find more than they expected.

"I joined dreading every work presentation. Two years later, I volunteer for them."

Meetings

Two hours that go faster than you expect.

Every meeting follows the same running order, but the content is different each time.

1
Warm up · 15 min
The warm-up
Everyone gets 15 seconds on a shared topic. Guests can simply say "pass" — no one will mind, and no one will notice.
2
Prepared speeches · 30–45 min
Prepared speeches
Three or four members deliver talks they've been working on — anything from a first nervous two-minute speech to a polished, funny, moving ten-minute performance. The range is part of what makes it so compelling to watch.
3
Break · 15 min
The break
15 minutes to grab a drink, meet other members, and decompress. For first-time visitors, this is the best time to ask questions — everyone is approachable and happy to chat.
4
Evaluations · 20 min
Evaluations
Each speaker receives a structured evaluation from a designated member — two minutes of thoughtful, kind, and genuinely useful feedback. It's one of the things members say they value most.
5
Table topics · 20 min
Table Topics — speaking off the cuff
A topic is announced and you've got thirty seconds to think before you speak for up to two minutes. This is the one part of the evening guests can join in too — but only if you've told us you'd like to, so you're never put on the spot. Most find themselves quietly desperate to have a go within a visit or two anyway.
🍺
Drinks afterwards
After the meeting most people head to a nearby pub — soft drinks and coffee very much included. It's optional, but this is consistently where first-time visitors decide they're coming back.

Your first visit

We're here to help.

You already know the agenda. Here's what it actually feels like to walk in as a guest.

7:15

Finding your way in

We run from 7:15 to 9:45 pm at Woking United Reformed Church, a 5-minute walk from the station, with town-centre car parks nearby. A member will be watching for you at the entrance — you won't have to find your own way to a room full of strangers.

👋

You'll have someone with you

The member who greets you sits with you for the evening. You're briefly introduced to the room — no speech, no spotlight — and they're your go-to for any questions, including during the break.

🙅

There's no pressure to speak

Guests are never put on the spot. If you're asked anything and don't fancy it, "pass" is a complete answer. Most first-timers just watch — that's exactly what the seat is for.

🍺

Stay as long as you like

Slip away after the meeting, or join the group for a drink nearby afterwards — entirely your call, no expectations either way. And come back as many times as you need before deciding anything.

FAQs

The questions everyone Googles first.

Honest answers to the things people want to know before they feel brave enough to ask.

Do I have to speak on my first visit?

Absolutely not. The most you'll be asked for is your name at the start, and even that's optional. The 15-second warm-up round is open to guests, but "pass" is a perfectly good answer. Nothing else is expected of you until you decide you're ready.

How many times can I come as a guest?

As many times as you need. There's no awkward moment where someone corners you about joining. We'd genuinely rather you come back five times and be certain than rush into something that doesn't fit.

What does membership cost?

Guest visits are always free. Membership is a £20 one-off joining fee, then £13 a month (billed in six-month blocks of £78). For what you get — structured development, mentoring, and a community — most members consider it exceptional value. Full breakdown in the Join section.

How do I join?

Come to a meeting, then join and pay online from our join page whenever you're ready — it takes a couple of minutes. No paper form, no interview, no audition.

When do I give my first speech?

Usually within your first few meetings as a member — you'll typically be scheduled about four meetings ahead. Need more time? Just say so. Your VP Education will work with you, not against you.

What if I'm terrified of my first speech?

You will be. So was everyone else. Your mentor will help you prepare it, your club will root for you while you're up there, and your evaluator will tell you what was great about it before anything else. First speeches at Woking Speakers are remembered fondly, not cringed at.
